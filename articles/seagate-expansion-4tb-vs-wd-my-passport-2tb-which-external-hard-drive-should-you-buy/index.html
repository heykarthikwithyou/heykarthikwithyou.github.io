Seagate Expansion 4TB vs WD My Passport 2TB: Which External Hard Drive Should You Buy?
Seagate Expansion 4TB vs WD My Passport 2TB

When your laptop or desktop starts running out of storage, an external hard drive remains one of the simplest and most affordable ways to add several terabytes of space. Two popular options are the Seagate Expansion 4TB Desktop External HDD and the WD My Passport 2TB Portable External Hard Disk Drive.

Both use USB connectivity and work with Windows and Mac computers, but they are designed with different priorities in mind. The Seagate Expansion offers 4TB of storage, making it particularly attractive for people who have large collections of videos, photos, documents and backups. The WD My Passport, on the other hand, has 2TB of storage but a much more compact portable design, along with password protection and hardware encryption.

So, which one is the better buy?

Let's compare the two external hard drives in terms of storage, portability, connectivity, features, convenience and overall value.

The Seagate Expansion 4TB is the more storage-focused option of the two. The STKP4000400 provides 4TB of capacity, which is twice what the WD My Passport 2TB offers.

That difference becomes significant if you regularly deal with large files. A collection of movies, family videos, photographs, software backups or work projects can quickly consume 1TB or 2TB. Having 4TB available means you don't have to start moving older files to another drive as quickly.

The drive uses USB 3.0 and comes with a USB cable and an 18W power adapter. This is an important distinction: the Seagate Expansion Desktop is designed to stay on a desk rather than being carried around with a laptop.

Seagate's specifications also list Rescue Data Recovery Services with this model. The APAC version of the STKP4000400 is listed with a three-year limited warranty and Rescue coverage, although buyers should verify the exact coverage applicable to their individual retail unit and region.

The Seagate is particularly useful as a:

  • Desktop backup drive
  • Photo and video storage drive
  • Movie and media library
  • PC or Mac backup destination
  • Long-term secondary storage
  • Storage drive for large files

Its biggest advantage is simple: 4TB gives you considerably more room to work with.

The downside is equally straightforward. Because it is a desktop drive with a separate power adapter, it isn't nearly as convenient to carry around as the WD My Passport.

The WD My Passport takes a very different approach. It is a compact portable hard drive designed to be carried between computers.

The current 2TB Black My Passport model listed by Western Digital measures approximately 107.19 x 74.93 x 13.46mm and weighs around 140 grams. It connects through a Micro-B connector and supports USB 3.2 Gen 1 as well as USB 2.0.

That makes it much easier to keep in a laptop bag, backpack or even a small accessory pouch.

For students, photographers, office users and people who regularly move between home and work, this portability can matter more than having an additional 2TB of capacity.

The WD My Passport also has an advantage when it comes to data security. Western Digital lists password protection and 256-bit AES hardware encryption, along with included device-management software with ransomware-defense features.

That's a meaningful benefit if you're carrying personal documents, work files or other information that shouldn't be easily accessible if the drive is lost.

Storage Capacity

This is probably the easiest category to judge.

The Seagate Expansion has 4TB, while the WD My Passport has 2TB.

If storage capacity is your main priority, the Seagate is the clear winner.

Four terabytes can be particularly useful for people who regularly store high-resolution photographs, large video files, downloaded media, backups or multiple computer images.

The WD's 2TB capacity is still enough for everyday use, but heavy users may eventually find themselves needing another storage device.

Winner: Seagate Expansion 4TB

Portability

Here the result is reversed.

The WD My Passport is specifically designed to be portable. It is small, lightweight and USB powered, so you don't need to find a power socket every time you want to access your files.

The Seagate Expansion Desktop is considerably larger and weighs around 1.176kg according to Seagate's specifications. It also requires an external 18W power adapter.

You can certainly move it when necessary, but that's not what it is designed for.

If you want an external hard drive that stays beside your laptop or desktop, this doesn't matter much. If you're travelling frequently, the WD is much more practical.

Winner: WD My Passport 2TB

Connectivity and Compatibility

Both drives are designed to work with Windows and Mac computers.

The Seagate Expansion requires an available USB 3.0 port for USB 3.0 transfer speeds, while Seagate notes that reformatting may be required for use with Apple's Time Machine.

The WD My Passport supports USB 3.2 Gen 1 and USB 2.0 and is also compatible with Windows and Mac systems. Western Digital lists a transfer interface rate of up to 5Gb/s.

Neither should be purchased expecting SSD-like performance. These are traditional mechanical hard drives, so they are primarily about affordable high-capacity storage, rather than extremely fast file transfers.

Security

This is one of the areas where the WD My Passport stands out.

Its password protection and 256-bit AES hardware encryption make it more appealing to users who travel with sensitive information.

The Seagate's biggest selling point is instead its capacity and included Rescue Data Recovery Services on the specified model.

It's worth remembering that neither feature means you should keep your only copy of important files on the drive. External HDDs can fail, regardless of brand. Important photographs, documents and work files should ideally have another backup.

Which One Should You Buy?

The answer depends largely on how you intend to use the drive.

Choose the Seagate Expansion 4TB if you want maximum storage for the money and expect the drive to remain mostly on your desk. It is the better choice for large photo collections, video archives, desktop backups, movies and other files that consume a lot of space.

Choose the WD My Passport 2TB if portability is more important. Its compact design, USB-powered operation and built-in security features make it a better companion for laptops and people who frequently move their data between locations.

For a home desktop backup setup, we would pick the Seagate Expansion 4TB. The extra 2TB is substantial, and the desktop-oriented design isn't a disadvantage when the drive is going to stay in one place.

For students, travellers and laptop users, the WD My Passport 2TB is the more convenient choice. It sacrifices capacity for portability and adds useful security features.

Final Verdict

Seagate Expansion 4TB – Better for Capacity

The Seagate Expansion 4TB is the stronger overall option if your priority is large-capacity storage. Four terabytes gives it a major advantage over the WD My Passport 2TB, particularly for users storing videos, photographs, backups and other large files. The included Rescue Data Recovery Services on the specified model also add an extra layer of reassurance.

WD My Passport 2TB – Better for Portability

The WD My Passport 2TB is the better option if you want something small, lightweight and easy to carry. Its password protection and 256-bit AES hardware encryption are valuable additions for users who take their storage drive outside the home or office.

Our Pick: Seagate Expansion 4TB

For most buyers comparing these exact two models, the Seagate Expansion 4TB gets our overall recommendation because its 4TB capacity gives it a significant practical advantage. If the drive is primarily going to sit on a desk and serve as a backup or media-storage drive, the extra capacity is more useful than the WD's portability.

However, if you frequently carry your external drive with your laptop, don't automatically choose the Seagate. In that situation, the WD My Passport 2TB is the smarter and more convenient purchase.

Bottom line: Buy the Seagate Expansion 4TB for capacity and desktop storage; choose the WD My Passport 2TB for portability and security.
