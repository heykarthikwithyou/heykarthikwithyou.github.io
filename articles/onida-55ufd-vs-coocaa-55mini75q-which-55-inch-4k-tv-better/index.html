Onida 55UFD vs Coocaa 55MINI75Q: Which 55-Inch 4K TV Is Better?

Compare picture quality, smart features, sound, gaming, pricing and overall value to find the better 55-inch 4K TV.

Onida 55UFD vs Coocaa 55MINI75Q: Which 55-Inch 4K TV Is Better?
Onida 55UFD vs Coocaa 55MINI75Q

The Onida 55UFD and Coocaa 55MINI75Q are both 55-inch 4K smart TVs, but they are aimed at slightly different buyers. While Onida focuses on affordable 4K entertainment with Fire TV, Coocaa brings Mini LED, QLED, 120Hz and advanced HDR features at a higher price.

A 55-inch 4K TV is no longer something you need to spend a fortune on. There are several options available around the ₹30,000–₹40,000 price range, but the specifications can vary quite a bit once you look beyond screen size and resolution.

The Onida 55UFD is one such option. It gives you a 55-inch 4K LED display, Fire TV, HDR support and the usual smart-TV connectivity at a price that keeps it in the more affordable part of the segment.

The Coocaa 55MINI75Q, meanwhile, takes things up a notch. It comes with a Mini LED display, QLED technology, local dimming, 120Hz refresh rate, Dolby Vision, HDR10+ and Google TV.

The price difference between the two isn't huge, so the question is whether the Coocaa's extra display and gaming features are worth paying for.

Here's how the two compare.

1. Display: Coocaa Has the More Advanced Panel

Both TVs offer a 55-inch 4K display, so you get the same basic 3840 × 2160 resolution from either one.

The difference is in the display technology.

The Onida 55UFD uses a conventional LED display and has a 60Hz refresh rate. That's fairly standard for a TV in this price range and should be more than enough for everyday viewing, including TV channels, YouTube, OTT platforms and movies. Current listings also mention HDR10 support.

The Coocaa 55MINI75Q is considerably more ambitious. It uses a Mini LED display with QLED technology, local dimming and a 120Hz refresh rate. Coocaa also lists 400 nits of brightness and a 3000:1 contrast ratio for the model.

Mini LED and local dimming are particularly useful when watching content with a mixture of very bright and very dark areas. Instead of treating the entire backlight in the same way, local dimming allows the TV to control different areas of the screen more independently.

For everyday viewing, the Onida is perfectly capable. But if display quality is high on your list, the Coocaa has the stronger hardware.

Winner: Coocaa 55MINI75Q

2. Picture Quality: Coocaa Is Better Suited to Movies

The difference becomes more obvious when you start watching good-quality HDR content.

The Onida's 4K LED panel can produce a sharp picture, and HDR10 support gives compatible content a wider range of brightness and contrast. For regular OTT streaming and television viewing, there's little reason to complain.

The Coocaa has considerably more to work with. Its Mini LED backlighting is combined with QLED technology and local dimming, while Dolby Vision and HDR10+ are also supported.

This makes the Coocaa more appealing if you regularly watch movies and shows mastered in HDR.

The difference won't necessarily be dramatic with a low-quality TV channel or compressed YouTube video. The better display hardware becomes more useful when the source itself is good.

If you mostly watch regular TV and streaming content, the Onida should do the job. If you have a growing collection of 4K movies or regularly watch Dolby Vision and HDR content, the Coocaa is the better fit.

Winner: Coocaa 55MINI75Q

3. Refresh Rate: 120Hz Makes a Difference

The Onida 55UFD has a 60Hz refresh rate, while the Coocaa 55MINI75Q supports 120Hz.

For movies and normal television programmes, 60Hz isn't necessarily a problem. Most people aren't going to notice the refresh rate while watching a regular drama or sitcom.

It becomes more useful with fast-moving content.

Sports, action scenes and games can look smoother on a 120Hz display. Coocaa also includes features such as ALLM and MEMC, with the TV positioned more clearly as an entertainment and gaming-oriented model.

This is one of those specifications you may not care about today but could appreciate later, especially if you're planning to connect a modern gaming console.

Winner: Coocaa 55MINI75Q

4. Gaming: Coocaa Is the Better Choice

If gaming is an important reason for buying a new TV, the Coocaa is the easier recommendation.

Its 120Hz panel is a major advantage over the Onida's 60Hz display. The Coocaa also supports ALLM and MEMC, while its gaming features are designed around the higher refresh rate. Current documentation also lists VRR and a Game Bar among its gaming features.

That doesn't mean the Onida cannot be used for gaming. It can handle casual console gaming without a problem.

But if you have a PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S or a gaming PC and want to take advantage of smoother motion, the Coocaa makes more sense.

Winner: Coocaa 55MINI75Q

5. Smart TV Experience: Fire TV vs Google TV

The smart-TV experience is where things become more subjective.

The Onida 55UFD runs Fire TV, which makes it particularly attractive if you already use Amazon's ecosystem. Alexa integration and the familiar Fire TV interface are useful for finding content and controlling compatible smart devices.

The Coocaa uses Google TV. It supports popular apps including Netflix, Prime Video, JioHotstar and YouTube, and also brings Google Assistant and Chromecast functionality.

The Coocaa also has 2GB of RAM and 32GB of internal storage, giving it a useful amount of space for apps and smart-TV content.

If you already use Alexa and Amazon devices, Fire TV may feel more natural.

If you use Android phones, Google services and Chromecast regularly, Google TV will probably be the better fit.

There isn't a clear winner here. It really comes down to which ecosystem you prefer.

Winner: Tie

6. Sound: Coocaa Packs More Power

Sound isn't usually the first thing people look at when buying a TV, but it can make a significant difference if you aren't planning to buy a soundbar.

The Onida 55UFD has a 20W audio system. That's enough for regular viewing, news, TV shows and casual streaming, although a soundbar would be useful if you want more impact from movies.

The Coocaa offers 36W audio output and supports Dolby Atmos. Its specification sheet also lists Dolby and DTS audio support.

The additional output gives the Coocaa an advantage for movies and sports, particularly if you're using the TV's built-in speakers.

That said, anyone looking for a truly cinematic experience should still consider a soundbar or home-theatre system.

Winner: Coocaa 55MINI75Q

7. Connectivity: Both Cover the Basics

Neither TV leaves you short of the essential ports.

The Onida has three HDMI ports and two USB ports, along with Wi-Fi, Ethernet and Bluetooth connectivity.

The Coocaa also offers three HDMI ports and two USB ports, along with Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Ethernet. Its connectivity package also includes HDMI ARC/eARC support.

For most homes, that is enough to connect a set-top box, gaming console, soundbar and other accessories without constantly swapping cables.

Winner: Tie

The Onida 55UFD is aimed at buyers who don't want to spend too much on a large-screen TV.

Its 55-inch 4K LED display, 60Hz refresh rate, HDR10 support and Fire TV platform cover the basics well. You also get three HDMI ports, two USB ports and 20W audio.

The main compromise is that it doesn't offer the more advanced display technology found on the Coocaa. There's no Mini LED backlight or 120Hz panel, so buyers looking specifically for premium HDR performance or high-refresh-rate gaming should spend more.

But for someone who simply wants a large 4K Fire TV at a reasonable price, the Onida 55UFD makes a good deal of sense.

The Coocaa 55MINI75Q is the more interesting TV if picture quality is your priority.

It combines a 55-inch 4K Mini LED display with QLED technology, local dimming and a 120Hz refresh rate. It also supports Dolby Vision and HDR10+, while the Google TV platform provides access to the major streaming services.

The 36W audio system is another useful upgrade, and the TV's 2GB RAM and 32GB storage give the Google TV platform plenty of room for apps.

For movie watchers, sports fans and gamers, the Coocaa is the more complete package.

Onida 55UFD vs Coocaa 55MINI75Q: Which One Should You Buy?

The answer depends on what you expect from your next TV.

Choose the Onida 55UFD if:

  • You prefer Fire TV.
  • Most of your viewing is OTT streaming and regular television.
  • You don't need a 120Hz panel.
  • You want a straightforward 55-inch 4K TV.

Choose the Coocaa 55MINI75Q if:

  • Picture quality is important to you.
  • You watch a lot of HDR and 4K content.
  • You want Mini LED and QLED technology.
  • You play games on a console or PC.
  • You want a 120Hz display.
  • You prefer Google TV.

Final Verdict

The Onida 55UFD is the better choice for buyers who want to keep the budget under control. It gives you the essentials—a 55-inch 4K display, Fire TV, HDR support, useful connectivity and 20W audio—without pushing the price much beyond ₹30,000.

The Coocaa 55MINI75Q, however, is the better overall TV.

For roughly ₹5,000 more, you get a much more advanced display with Mini LED, QLED, local dimming and 120Hz, along with Dolby Vision, HDR10+, 36W audio and a more gaming-friendly feature set.

So, if you're simply looking for an affordable 55-inch 4K smart TV, go for the Onida 55UFD.

But if you're already spending around ₹30,000 and can stretch the budget a little further, the Coocaa 55MINI75Q is the one we'd pick. The better display and 120Hz panel make the additional investment easier to justify, particularly if you watch a lot of movies or plan to use the TV for gaming.

Our Pick: Coocaa 55MINI75Q
