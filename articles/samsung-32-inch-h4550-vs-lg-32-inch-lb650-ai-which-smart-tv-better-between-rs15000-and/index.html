Samsung 32-inch H4550 vs LG 32-inch LB650 AI: Which Smart TV Is Better Between ₹15,000 and ₹18,000?
Samsung 32-inch H4550 vs LG 32-inch LB650 AI

Buying a 32-inch smart TV in 2026 is no longer simply about finding the cheapest screen from a familiar brand. At this size, buyers can still get features such as HDR, modern smart TV platforms, screen mirroring, Bluetooth connectivity and app-based entertainment. The Samsung UA32H4550FUXXL and LG 32LB650BPLA are two models that sit squarely in this category, but they take noticeably different approaches.

Samsung's H4550 focuses on its Tizen smart TV platform, picture processing and stronger built-in audio, while LG's 2026 LB650 puts more emphasis on AI-powered features, webOS 26 and newer smart TV functionality. Both use an HD 1366 x 768 resolution on an approximately 32-inch screen, so the real differences are elsewhere.

Display Quality: Similar Resolution, Different Processing

There isn't a major resolution difference between these two TVs. Both offer an HD resolution of 1366 x 768 pixels, which is fairly standard for 32-inch televisions in this price segment.

Samsung uses its Hyper Real picture engine along with HDR, HDR10+ support, PurColor and Contrast Enhancer. Samsung says these technologies are designed to improve colour reproduction, contrast and the overall sense of depth in images.

LG takes a newer processing approach with its α5 AI Processor Gen9. The LB650 also supports HDR10/HLG, AI upscaling and dynamic tone mapping. The processor is intended to improve sharpness and picture depth, particularly when the source isn't already high resolution.

For regular DTH channels, YouTube, streaming services and casual movie watching, both should be considered HD smart TV options rather than televisions designed for 4K-level picture detail.

Smart TV Experience: Tizen vs webOS 26

This is where the two models become more interesting.

Samsung's H4550 runs on Tizen and includes features such as Samsung TV Plus, Bixby, Universal Guide, Apple AirPlay and mobile-to-TV functions. It also supports SmartThings-related functionality and wireless screen experiences.

LG's LB650 runs webOS 26 and adds a considerably newer set of AI-oriented features. LG lists an AI Hub, AI Concierge, AI Chatbot, My Page, LG Channels, Google Cast, Apple AirPlay and LG ThinQ compatibility. It also supports a full web browser.

The LG is therefore particularly interesting for buyers who want a newer smart TV interface and are curious about AI-assisted features. Samsung, meanwhile, offers the advantage of an established Tizen ecosystem and Samsung-specific services.

Sound: Samsung Has a Clear Specification Advantage

Sound is one of the biggest differences between these televisions.

The Samsung UA32H4550FUXXL is specified with 20W RMS audio output, a 2-channel speaker system, Adaptive Sound, Q-Symphony and OTS Lite. Samsung's OTS Lite is designed to create a more immersive virtual surround effect by making sound appear to follow the action on screen.

LG's 32LB650BPLA has a 10W 2.0-channel audio system. It adds AI Sound Pro, Clear Voice Pro and Sound Sync, with LG stating that AI Sound Pro can virtually up-mix audio.

On paper, Samsung has twice the listed audio output. That doesn't automatically mean it will sound twice as good, because speaker tuning and room acoustics matter, but buyers who intend to use the television without a soundbar should pay attention to this difference.

Connectivity

Connectivity is relatively well matched.

Samsung provides two HDMI ports, one USB port, Bluetooth, eARC and Apple AirPlay.

LG also provides two HDMI inputs, one USB 2.0 port, Bluetooth 5.3, Wi-Fi 5, Ethernet and eARC. LG additionally lists Google Cast and smartphone remote functionality through LG ThinQ.

For a typical bedroom or living-room setup involving a set-top box, gaming console or streaming device, neither TV is particularly restrictive. Two HDMI ports should cover most basic setups, although buyers with several external devices may eventually want an HDMI switch or soundbar with pass-through.

AI Features: LG Takes the Newer Approach

The biggest talking point around the LG 32LB650BPLA is its AI-focused smart TV experience.

LG lists AI Hub, AI Concierge, AI Chatbot, AI Voice ID readiness and Multi-AI search capabilities on the 2026 model. Some of these experiences depend on compatible accessories or services, so buyers should not assume that every AI function works identically out of the box.

Samsung's UA32H4550FUXXL is less focused on AI branding but still provides Bixby and compatibility with voice assistants, alongside its Tizen ecosystem.

So the distinction is fairly straightforward: LG is pushing newer AI-driven television features, while Samsung concentrates more on its established smart TV ecosystem and picture/audio technologies.

Design and Size

Both TVs are designed around the familiar compact 32-inch format, making them suitable for bedrooms, smaller living rooms, guest rooms or spaces where a larger television would be unnecessary.

Samsung lists a bezel-less three-side design and a slim profile. Its dimensions without the stand are approximately 716.4 x 430.5 x 78.7 mm.

LG lists dimensions of 719 x 430 x 71 mm without the stand, making the LG slightly wider but slimmer according to the manufacturer's specifications. It also supports a 100 x 100 mm VESA mounting pattern.

For wall mounting, checking the actual mounting position and bracket compatibility before installation is still advisable.

There is also an important point that is easy to overlook: neither TV moves beyond HD resolution. If your priority is maximum picture sharpness for 4K streaming, PC use or gaming, a 4K television would be a more appropriate category to explore. But for everyday DTH viewing, streaming, YouTube and casual entertainment on a 32-inch screen, HD remains a practical choice.

Final Verdict

The Samsung UA32H4550FUXXL and LG 32LB650BPLA are both compelling 32-inch smart TV options, but they appeal to slightly different buyers.

Samsung's strengths are its 20W audio system, HDR10+ support, Tizen platform, PurColor, OTS Lite and Samsung ecosystem. LG counters with a newer 2026 platform, α5 AI Processor Gen9, webOS 26, HDR10/HLG, AI-powered features and broader modern casting and smart-home functionality.

For shoppers who mainly want a compact TV with stronger built-in sound and Samsung's familiar smart TV environment, the H4550 has plenty to offer. Those who are more interested in LG's newer webOS 26 interface and AI-focused features may find the LB650 more interesting.

Ultimately, both models cover the fundamentals well. The deciding factors are less about screen size or resolution—which are very similar—and more about sound, smart TV platform, AI features and the ecosystem you already use at home.
