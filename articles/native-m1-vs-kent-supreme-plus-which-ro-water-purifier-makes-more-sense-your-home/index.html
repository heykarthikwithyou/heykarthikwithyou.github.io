Native M1 vs KENT Supreme Plus: Which RO Water Purifier Makes More Sense for Your Home?

Choose the right RO water purifier based on purification, maintenance, storage, features and long-term ownership.

By Karthik Kumar D K Published: Aug 31, 2026 9:08 AM IST

Native M1 vs KENT Supreme Plus: Which RO Water Purifier Makes More Sense for Your Home?
Native M1 vs KENT Supreme Plus

Buying an RO water purifier in India can be surprisingly confusing. Most models seem to promise the same things: multiple purification stages, mineral enrichment, copper, alkaline water, large storage tanks and better-tasting drinking water. Once you start comparing models, however, the important differences are often not immediately obvious.

The Native M1 by Urban Company and the KENT Supreme Plus Alkaline+Copper are good examples. Both are premium RO water purifiers with multiple purification technologies and 8-litre storage tanks. But they are designed around slightly different priorities.

The Native M1 puts considerable emphasis on reducing maintenance and making ownership simpler, while the KENT Supreme Plus takes a more traditional feature-rich RO approach, combining RO, UV, UF, TDS Control, alkaline and copper technologies.

So rather than asking which purifier has more features, the better question is: which one makes more sense for the water you receive and the way you want to maintain your purifier?

The First Thing to Understand: Your Water Matters More Than the Purifier

Before comparing the two machines, there is one decision that should come first: understanding your water source.

A home receiving municipal water may have a very different purification requirement from one relying on borewell or tanker water. TDS, hardness and other characteristics can vary considerably even between two homes in the same city.

This is why buying a purifier simply because it has more purification stages isn't necessarily the right approach.

Both Native M1 and KENT Supreme Plus use RO as a major part of their purification process. RO is particularly relevant when dissolved impurities and higher TDS levels are a concern.

KENT specifies the Supreme Plus for brackish, tap and municipal water, with a maximum operating feed-water TDS specification of 1500 mg/L. The manufacturer also recommends checking the characteristics of the incoming water before choosing the appropriate purification system.

Native M1 is similarly aimed at Indian household water conditions and uses RO alongside other purification technologies.

In other words, both are designed for situations where an RO purifier is actually required. If your incoming water has low TDS and doesn't need RO, buying an expensive RO purifier may not automatically be the best solution.

What Actually Happens to the Water?

This is where looking beyond the stage count becomes useful.

Think of an RO purifier as a sequence rather than a number printed on the product box.

Water enters the purifier, passes through filtration and purification stages, and then goes through additional treatment before being stored for consumption.

The Native M1 uses a 10-stage RO+UV purification system with alkaline, copper and mineraliser-related treatment. The emphasis is on combining different technologies while also maintaining the mineral profile of the treated water.

The KENT Supreme Plus uses RO+UV+UF along with alkaline, copper and TDS Control technologies. KENT's Mineral RO technology is intended to reduce dissolved impurities while allowing control over the TDS of the output water.

Neither approach is automatically better simply because one has a larger stage count.

For a buyer, the more useful distinction is this: KENT gives you a broader combination of RO, UV and UF with TDS control, while Native M1 puts considerable emphasis on its overall purification system and low-maintenance ownership.

Native M1's Biggest Selling Point Isn't Actually the 10 Stages

If you are comparing these two purifiers, the feature that deserves the most attention on the Native M1 isn't the "10-stage" label.

It is the maintenance proposition.

Urban Company markets Native M1 around a 2-year filter life and says its Smart Rinse Technology helps extend filter performance. The purifier also comes with a 2-year unconditional warranty under the stated warranty terms.

This changes the ownership conversation.

With many RO purifiers, the initial purchase price is only one part of the expense. Over several years, buyers also have to think about filter replacements, membrane changes and service visits.

Native M1 attempts to reduce that recurring maintenance burden.

That makes it particularly interesting for someone who doesn't want to keep track of annual purifier servicing or spend regularly on replacement filters.

Of course, actual filter life depends on factors such as incoming water quality and household consumption. A home using heavily contaminated or high-hardness water can put more demand on a purifier than another home with relatively cleaner input water.

KENT Supreme Plus Takes a Different Route

KENT doesn't try to make the purifier's maintenance story the central attraction.

Instead, the Supreme Plus focuses heavily on purification features and conventional RO functionality.

You get RO, UV and UF purification, along with alkaline and copper technologies and TDS Control. The purifier also has an 8-litre storage tank and a stated purification capacity of up to 20 litres per hour.

Another useful feature is its automatic flushing system. RO membranes can accumulate contaminants over time, so automatically flushing the membrane can help maintain its operation.

There is also UV protection inside the storage tank, providing another layer of protection for the stored water.

For someone who looks at a purifier and thinks, "I want as many relevant purification and control features as possible," the KENT Supreme Plus has a strong argument.

What About the 8-Litre Tanks?

Interestingly, there isn't much to separate these two on storage capacity.

Both have 8-litre storage tanks, which is a practical capacity for a typical household.

The importance of storage becomes more obvious during periods when the purifier isn't actively producing water. If several family members need drinking water at the same time, having treated water already stored means you don't necessarily have to wait for the purification process to finish.

For a small or medium-sized family, 8 litres is generally a comfortable middle ground.

The bigger difference is therefore not storage capacity but how each purifier approaches purification and ownership.

Which One Makes More Sense for Borewell or Tanker Water?

This is where you should be slightly careful about choosing a winner.

Borewell and tanker water can vary substantially in TDS, hardness and other characteristics. Simply saying that one purifier is "best for borewell water" without knowing the actual water quality would be misleading.

KENT specifically positions the Supreme Plus for brackish, tap and municipal water and provides operating specifications for the purifier.

Native M1 is also designed for Indian household water conditions and uses RO purification.

If you depend on borewell or tanker water, test your water first. A basic water-quality assessment can tell you considerably more about the purifier you need than a comparison of marketing terms.

Where Native M1 Has the Stronger Argument

The Native M1 becomes particularly compelling when maintenance is high on your list of priorities.

Its 2-year filter-life proposition and 2-year unconditional warranty are designed to address one of the biggest frustrations with RO ownership: recurring service and filter expenses.

For someone who wants to install a purifier and worry less about routine maintenance, this is a meaningful advantage.

It also makes the Native M1 interesting for households where convenience matters more than having every possible purification feature listed on the specification sheet.

Where KENT Supreme Plus Makes More Sense

The KENT Supreme Plus is more appealing if you prefer a feature-rich conventional RO system.

Its combination of RO, UV, UF, TDS Control, alkaline and copper technologies gives buyers a broad set of purification and water-treatment features.

Its 20-litre-per-hour purification rate is another advantage for households that expect relatively frequent water consumption.

And for buyers who are already comfortable with the KENT ecosystem, the brand's established presence in the Indian water-purifier market can be an important consideration when thinking about after-sales support.

Native M1 vs KENT Supreme Plus: The Final Decision

There isn't one universal winner here.

If your priority is lower maintenance, longer filter life and a longer warranty, the Native M1 by Urban Company is the more compelling choice.

If you want RO+UV+UF purification, TDS Control, alkaline and copper features, automatic flushing and a 20-LPH purification rate, the KENT Supreme Plus deserves a closer look.

For most buyers who simply want a reliable RO purifier without wanting to think too much about maintenance, we'd give the Native M1 a slight overall edge because its ownership proposition is unusually strong.

But if your priority is purification flexibility and a more traditional feature-rich RO setup, KENT Supreme Plus may be the better fit.

Ultimately, don't choose between these two just by counting purification stages. Choose based on your water source, TDS, household consumption and how much maintenance you are willing to deal with over the next few years.

That is what turns a good water purifier purchase into the right one for your home.

Is Native M1 better than KENT Supreme Plus?

Native M1 is better suited to buyers who prioritise low maintenance, long filter life and warranty coverage. KENT Supreme Plus is more suitable for buyers who prioritise a broad combination of RO, UV, UF, TDS Control, alkaline and copper technologies.

Does KENT Supreme Plus have TDS Control?

Yes. TDS Control is one of the features offered by the KENT Supreme Plus, allowing control over the TDS level of the purified water.

How much water can the Native M1 store?

The Native M1 has an 8-litre storage capacity.

How much water can KENT Supreme Plus purify per hour?

KENT specifies a purification capacity of up to 20 litres per hour for the Supreme Plus.

Which is better for a family?

Both can work well for a typical family. The decision should depend more on the household's water quality, consumption and maintenance preferences than simply on family size.

Should I buy an RO purifier for municipal water?

Not necessarily. Municipal water quality can vary, and RO isn't required for every municipal water supply. Check the TDS and other characteristics of your incoming water before choosing an RO purifier.
